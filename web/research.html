04 · BROADER RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

Five-level personalization threshold

Insufficient evidence

The levels are a conceptual decision framework. The available secondary data do not measure valid engagement and direct intrusion responses at every level on the same exposure.

What this means: Available data do not measure direct intrusion and engagement across all five levels on the same exposure, so a validated threshold is not supported.

    Current decision: ——Needed for validation: direct intrusion labels, engagement outcomes, per-level exposure variation and held-out validation on compatible records. No level is recommended.
    Methodology & Limits

    What the linter does. It applies a fixed, rule-based rubric to visible copy, OCR text and user-provided targeting details to flag selected privacy and pressure cues. A score is a cue count, not a probability, measured reaction or validated consumer intrusiveness threshold.

    What the benchmarks mean. Survey results summarize separate research cohorts and general-ad responses. They provide context for similar cues; they do not represent this campaign's audience, rate an uploaded creative or predict an individual's reaction. The engagement datasets measure recorded actions or visits, not intrusiveness.

    What the image check covers. OCR reads printed text. It does not assess image layout, visual salience or whether an ad obstructs page content.

    What the planner calculates. Budget and break-even figures are arithmetic from the values entered. They are scenarios, not predicted uplift, sales or return on investment. Review permissions and eligibility independently before launch.

    Evidence limits. The datasets use different samples, measures and scales; the available data do not validate a five-level personalization threshold. Refer to each source and result above before drawing conclusions.
